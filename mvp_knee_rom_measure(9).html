How it works. The camera identifies hip, knee, and ankle landmarks — equivalent to a goniometer placed on the greater trochanter, lateral femoral epicondyle, and lateral malleolus. The angle is computed in a 3D body-relative frame: 0° = straight leg, positive = flexion, negative = hyperextension. Capture each endpoint when the reading is stable.

For clinical use, validate against a manual goniometer. Camera-based ROM has typical ±3–5° agreement with goniometry under good conditions.
